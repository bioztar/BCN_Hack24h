# ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — AI management systems (AIMS)

> **Source:** ISO/IEC 42001:2023, © ISO/IEC — <https://www.iso.org/standard/42001>.
> Pointer file: `corpus/standards/iso-iec-42001.md`.
> **Copyright:** no standard text is reproduced here. Everything below is an **authored
> summary**; clause and Annex A numbers are cited so a licensed reader can verify.
> **Type:** voluntary, certifiable management-system standard. **Last reviewed:** 2026-10-09.

## In plain language

ISO 42001 does for AI what ISO 27001 does for security: it asks you to run AI as a managed
system. Decide which AI systems are in scope and what role you play (building, providing,
or using AI), assess the risks *and* the impacts on people and society, choose controls,
document data provenance and model lifecycle, tell users what they need to know, keep
humans in charge where it matters, and get audited. It is the first certifiable AI
governance standard, and it pairs naturally with an existing ISO 27001 programme.

## 1. Scope

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who | Any organisation that develops, provides or uses AI systems — any size, sector, country. |
| Role awareness | Organisation determines its role(s) regarding AI systems (e.g. provider, producer, customer, partner, subject) as part of context. | 
| What | An AIMS over a defined scope (clause 4.3) of AI systems and activities. |
| Obligation status | Voluntary; binding via contract/tender. Not (as of 2026-10-09) a harmonised standard under the EU AI Act — VERIFY before claiming presumption of conformity. |

## 3. Annex A control groups (authored descriptions)

Annex A lists reference control objectives and controls (38 controls: A.2:3, A.3:2, A.4:5, A.5:4, A.6:9,
A.7:5, A.8:4, A.9:3, A.10:3); Annex B gives implementation guidance; Annex C lists potential
AI objectives and risk sources; Annex D covers use across domains.

| Group | Theme (authored description) | Product evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A.2 | Policies related to AI — AI policy, alignment with other policies, review | Published AI policy, review log |
| A.3 | Internal organisation — AI roles/responsibilities, reporting of concerns | RACI, concern-reporting channel |
| A.4 | Resources for AI systems — documentation of data, tooling, compute, human resources | Resource inventory per AI system |
| A.5 | Assessing impacts of AI systems — process, documentation, impacts on individuals and on society | Impact-assessment records |
| A.6 | AI system life cycle — objectives for responsible development, requirements, design, verification & validation, deployment, operation & monitoring, technical documentation, event logs | Model cards, eval reports, release gates, monitoring dashboards, event logs |

## 5. Enforcement mechanics

No statutory penalties. Major nonconformities block or suspend certification; contractual
consequences where customers require certification. Check the certification body's
accreditation for ISO/IEC 42001 specifically.

## 6. Mapping onto a software product

| Area | What an auditor will look for | Clause / Annex A |
|---|---|---|
| AI inventory | Register of AI systems in scope with role, intended use, owner, risk level | 4.3, A.4, A.9 |
| Data handling | Training/eval data provenance, quality criteria, preparation steps, PII handling | A.7 |
| Logging | Event logs for AI systems sufficient for monitoring and incident analysis | A.6 (operation & event logs) |
| Access control | Inherited from ISMS (ISO 27001 A.5.15–5.18, A.8.2–8.5) for models, weights, prompts, datasets | — (via 27001) |
| Incident response | AI incident definition (harmful output, drift, misuse), communication to interested parties | A.8, 10.2 |
| Human oversight | Defined human roles in the loop, override/stop capability, competence of overseers | A.9, 7.2, A.6 (deployment/operation) |
| Testing | Verification and validation criteria before release; red-team/evals; re-validation on change | A.6 (V&V) |
| Transparency | User-facing documentation of capabilities, limits, appropriate use | A.8 |
| Vendor management | Foundation-model / API supplier assessment; allocation of responsibilities in contracts | A.10 |
| Impact assessment | Documented AI system impact assessment per system, refreshed on change | 6.1.4, 8.4, A.5 |

## 7. Interplay

- **ISO 27001:** same Harmonized Structure (clauses 4–10) — integrate into one management system; reuse internal audit and management review.
- **NIST AI RMF:** GOVERN ≈ clauses 5, 7, A.2–A.3; MAP ≈ 4, 6.1.4, A.5; MEASURE ≈ 9.1, A.6 V&V; MANAGE ≈ 6.1.3, 8, 10. See `analysis/overlap-matrix.md`.
- **GDPR:** AI impact assessment (6.1.4) can share inputs with a GDPR Art. 35 DPIA but is not a substitute.
- **EU AI Act:** ISO 42001 supports — but does not by itself satisfy — AI-Act quality-management and risk-management duties. For classification and obligations use **`eu-ai-act-compliance`** / <https://github.com/bioztar/EU-AI-Act>.

## 8. Common misconceptions

- "42001 certifies our model is safe." — It certifies a management system, not a model.
- "42001 = AI Act compliance." — No presumption of conformity unless/until cited as a harmonised standard.
- "Only AI builders need it." — The standard covers organisations that *use* AI too.
