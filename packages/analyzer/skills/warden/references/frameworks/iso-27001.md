# ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — Information security management systems (ISMS)

> **Source:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (+ Amd 1:2024), © ISO/IEC — <https://www.iso.org/standard/27001>.
> Pointer file: `corpus/standards/iso-iec-27001.md`.
> **Copyright:** no standard text is reproduced here. Everything below is an **authored
> summary**; clause and Annex A control numbers are cited so a licensed reader can verify.
> **Type:** voluntary, certifiable management-system standard. **Last reviewed:** 2026-10-09.

## In plain language

ISO 27001 is the world's most recognised way to prove "we run security as a managed
system, not as heroics". You define what's in scope, assess your security risks, pick
controls to treat them (from a 93-control reference list), write down which you use and
why, run them, check they work, and get audited every year by an accredited certification
body. It's not law — but enterprise customers and many regulations treat the certificate
as strong evidence.

## 1. Scope

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who | Any organisation, any size, any sector, any country. |
| What | An ISMS over a **scope you define** (clause 4.3) — e.g. "the SaaS platform, its production environment and the teams operating it". Narrow scopes are allowed but customers read the scope statement. |
| Jurisdiction | None — international standard. Some regulators reference it (e.g. NIS2 Art. 25 encourages European/international standards). |
| Obligation status | Voluntary. Becomes binding through **contracts**, tenders, or certification commitments. |

## 5. Enforcement mechanics

No statutory penalties. Consequences are commercial and contractual:
- **Major nonconformity** at audit → certificate not issued, or suspended/withdrawn if not corrected in the CB's timeframe.
- Customer contracts / tenders may make certification a condition; loss can be a breach.
- Accreditation matters: certificates from non-accredited bodies (no IAF MLA signatory) carry less weight. Check the certificate on the CB's register / IAF CertSearch.

## 7. Interplay

- **NIS2 / DORA:** an ISO 27001 ISMS covers most of NIS2 Art. 21(2) and DORA Arts. 6–14 as
  *evidence*, but neither law accepts a certificate as automatic compliance — gaps typically
  remain in regulatory incident reporting clocks, management-body training and DORA Art. 30
  contract terms. See `analysis/overlap-matrix.md`.
- **GDPR:** supports Art. 32 security and Art. 24 accountability; privacy extension is ISO/IEC 27701.
- **EU AI Act:** cybersecurity of high-risk AI systems is an AI-Act topic — use
  **`eu-ai-act-compliance`** / <https://github.com/bioztar/EU-AI-Act>.

## 8. Common misconceptions

- "Certified = secure." — Certification attests a functioning management system over a scope, not absence of vulnerabilities.
- "We must implement all 93 controls." — All must be *considered*; exclusions with justification in the SoA are allowed (6.1.3).
- "The certificate covers the whole company." — Only the stated scope (4.3). Read it.
